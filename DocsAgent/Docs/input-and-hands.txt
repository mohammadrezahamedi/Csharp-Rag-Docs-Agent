Input and Hand Tracking Guide

This document explains how the player controls work in the VR project.

Controllers
The project uses the Unity Input System with the OpenXR plugin.
The trigger is used to grab objects and the grip button is used to hold weapons.
The A button on the right controller opens the pause menu.
The thumbstick on the left controller moves the player and the right thumbstick turns the camera.

Turning and Comfort
Snap turn is the default, with an angle of 45 degrees.
Smooth turn can be enabled in the comfort settings and runs at 90 degrees per second.
A vignette appears during smooth movement to reduce motion sickness.
Teleport movement is available as an alternative locomotion mode.

Hand Tracking
Controller-free hand tracking is supported on Quest 3 and Quest 2.
A pinch gesture between thumb and index finger acts like pressing the trigger.
A closed fist acts like pressing the grip button.
Hand tracking is automatically switched on when the player puts the controllers down for two seconds.
Tracking confidence below 0.5 makes the hand model fade out.

Haptics
Controller vibration is used for grabbing and hitting. The default amplitude is 0.4.
The maximum haptic duration for one pulse is 0.2 seconds.
Haptics are disabled automatically when the player uses hand tracking.

Grab System
Objects can be grabbed from up to 0.3 meters away using a sphere check.
Thrown objects inherit the controller velocity averaged over the last 5 frames.
